Robotic Urology

What Is Robotic Surgery?

Abdullah Harun Kınık, MD July 1, 2026 4 min read

Table of Contents

Overview How Does It Work? Advantages Uses in Urology Is Robotic Surgery Always the "Best" Method? Frequently Asked Questions

Overview

Robotic surgery (robot-assisted surgery) is a minimally invasive method in which robotic arms are directed by the surgeon from a console. The idea that "the robot performs the surgery on its own" is a misconception; the surgeon controls every movement. The system translates the surgeon's hand movements to the robotic instruments with a high degree of precision.

How Does It Work?

The surgeon sits at a console that provides a three-dimensional, magnified view and directs the robotic arms from there. The robotic instruments can have a greater range of motion than the human wrist, which allows for precise suturing and dissection in narrow, deep spaces. The procedure is carried out with thin instruments placed through small incisions.

Advantages

The general advantages of a minimally invasive approach also stand out in robotic surgery: small incisions, a three-dimensional and magnified view, and precise, tremor-free instrument movement. In suitable cases, these features can help provide a more detailed view of the surgical site and more controlled work. Every patient's recovery is unique, and outcomes vary based on the characteristics of both the condition and the patient.

Uses in Urology

The robotic approach can be used in a variety of urologic procedures. Choosing the appropriate method is determined by evaluating the type and extent of the condition together with the patient's overall status. Robotic surgery may not be necessary or appropriate for every patient and every situation; this decision is made through individual assessment.

Is Robotic Surgery Always the "Best" Method?

No. Robotic, open, and laparoscopic surgery can each be advantageous in different situations. What matters isn't the name of the method, but choosing the approach that's most appropriate and safe for the patient. For that reason, the treatment decision should be made together with the surgeon after a thorough evaluation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the robot perform the surgery on its own?

No, every movement is controlled by the surgeon; the robot is the surgeon's tool.

Is robotic surgery safer?

It offers minimally invasive advantages in suitable cases, but it isn't accurate to say it's "always better" — the decision is individual.

Is recovery faster?

Smaller incisions can make recovery easier in some cases; the outcome varies from person to person.
