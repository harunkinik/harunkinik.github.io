The Right Time for Circumcision

Abdullah Harun Kınık, MD June 2, 2026 4 min read

Table of Contents

Overview

Circumcision is the removal of the skin covering the tip of the penis (the foreskin). In Turkey it's commonly performed for cultural and religious reasons, and in some cases for medical reasons. There's no single answer to the question of "the right time" — the family's preference, the child's health status, and medical evaluation are all considered together.

Timing From a Medical Perspective

Absent a medical contraindication, circumcision can be performed at various ages. Commonly preferred periods are the newborn/early infancy period and later childhood. The period generally not recommended is roughly ages 3 to 6, when a child's body awareness and sense of privacy are developing; the procedure can have a greater psychological impact on the child during this window. For that reason, it's preferable to plan it either before or after this age range.

Situations Where Circumcision Should Be Postponed

In certain situations, circumcision should be postponed and a pediatric urologist consulted first. Hypospadias is chief among these, since the foreskin may be needed for a future repair. Buried penis, penile curvature, a history of bleeding disorders, and certain congenital differences also require evaluation first.

How Is It Done?

Circumcision is performed under appropriate conditions and with appropriate anesthesia. The method is determined based on the child's age and condition. Simple wound care and a short recovery period follow the procedure.

Recovery and What to Watch For

During recovery, it's recommended to keep the area clean, follow the recommended care routine, and stay alert for possible warning signs — excessive swelling, ongoing bleeding, fever, or difficulty urinating. A physician should be consulted if any of these occur.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the healthiest age?

Absent a medical contraindication, early infancy or later childhood are commonly preferred; ages 3–6 are generally not recommended.

Can circumcision be done if hypospadias is present?

Standard circumcision is not recommended; a pediatric urology evaluation is needed first.

Does circumcision offer health benefits?

Its effects on certain urinary tract and hygiene issues are debated; the decision should be made through individual assessment.
